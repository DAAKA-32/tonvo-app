## Angle A — POV: You Just Lost $50K

_Targets: ICP-1 (quota-slipping AE) primarily, ICP-2 secondary. Loss aversion. Reuse Ad11 master + reformat 1:1, plus statics + text-only._

### Hooks (1.5s, ≤8 words)
1. POV: you just lost a $50K deal. (8)
2. The deal you needed just ghosted you. (7)
3. Three signals you missed on that call. (7)

### Primary text (≤125 chars)
**A.** You hear the missed objection now. Tonvo hears it on the call. Live coaching only you can see. 30s setup. No card. _(123)_
**B.** The $50K deal didn't ghost you. You missed three signals. Tonvo catches all three live. Try free → tonvo.co _(112)_
**C.** Replay your last lost deal. Find the line that would've closed it. Then run Tonvo on the next call. Free, 30s. _(115)_

### Headlines (≤27 chars)
A. You lost a $50K deal. (21) · B. Hear the deal during it. (24) · C. Catch what you missed. (22)

### Descriptions (≤27 chars)
A. Live in-call copilot. (21) · B. Free starter. 30s setup. (24)

### CTA button
Try for Free

### Notes / tradeoffs
Run Hook 1 + Primary A + Headline A first. That's the existing Ad11 narrative, on-message and on-tone. Hook 2 is the strongest swipe-file lift (POV pattern 5, "ghosted" variant) and the one I'd A/B against the original — it pulls a different lever (anger rather than regret). Primary C is the riskiest because "replay your last lost deal" implies a feature we don't sell (call review is async — Tonvo is live). Use C only on text-only placements where the implied product action is ambiguous, or rewrite in v2. Headline C is at exact 27 chars; if Meta truncates with ellipsis on an unfamiliar font we lose "missed" — keep Headline B as the safer default.

## Concerns for strategist

1. **The "+27%" claim across A and B.** Ad10 already runs it and the strategy doc flags Meta auto-review as a possible failure mode. Every Angle B variant above leans on +27%; if review flags the claim, only Primary B (which puts +27% in body, not headline) survives without rewrite. Recommend prepping a no-number fallback for Angle B headlines: "Same pitch. Better close rate." (24 chars) — flatter, but ships.

2. **Angle C's "what's running in her browser" reveal.** This works in video where the cut to demo capture lands the punchline. In text-only and static placements, the line is vague — "what's running in her browser" could be anything. I patched this in the notes (append "(it's Tonvo)") but a cleaner solve is to re-cut Angle C copy as two distinct buckets: video-narrative (current copy works) and text-only (rewrite to name Tonvo earlier). Decide before media buyer locks placements.

3. **Angle E's invented 9% number in Primary B.** The strategy doc cites "8–12%" as ICP-3's typical demo-to-paid conversion — that's industry-pattern, not a Tonvo measurement. If we publish Primary B as written, we're implying we know the reader's specific number. Either soften ("single digits") or pull the variant. I left it in so the strategist can decide.

4. **Angle D Hook 1 ("Stop winging your demos") is the weakest hook in the whole set.** I kept it because the angle name is "Stop Winging It" and dropping the verbatim phrase from the hook list felt like flagging a problem with the angle name itself. If the strategist wants, rename the angle to "Stop Pitching From Memory" — Hook 3 in Angle D becomes the new lead and the angle title aligns with the strongest hook.

5. **No `[needs verification]` flagged on the "1,247 closers" proof line** because the brief and strategy doc both note it's confirmed-in-use on existing ads. If that number ever changes, every Angle B variant that uses it needs updating.

6. **CTA button split.** Angles A–D use `Try for Free` (consistent with existing Ad11/Ad10 CTA). Angle E uses `Sign Up` per the rationale in its notes. If the media buyer wants a single CTA across the campaign for cleaner attribution, default everything to `Try for Free` — Angle E loses ~5% click intent in my read but gains comparability.
