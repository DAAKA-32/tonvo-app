## Angle F · Without Taking a Single Note

_Mechanic per brief §3: cognitive offload. AE scrambling to type, missing the live moment. Pain is concurrent (right now her hands are doing it), not retrospective. Landing match: typewriter line 2 "Without taking a single note."_

### Category 1 — Statement-as-truth

- **F-S1.1 — "I stopped taking notes 3 weeks ago."**
  - Mode: audio-driven (TTS Edge, female AE voice, flat-confident, no upspeak). On-screen: minimal — single chrome serif line lower-third over keyboard B-roll going dark.
  - When to use: lead hook for V2-01 (per brief §7). The first-person flips the ad from "we sell you a thing" to "I am you, two weeks ahead." Reduces marketing-voice resistance.

- **F-S1.2 — "My hands haven't typed on a call since."**
  - Mode: audio-driven (same voice as F-S1.1, continuation logic — sequel hook for the audience that saw the first).
  - When to use: V2-08 static-frame variant, or as the S0 in a re-targeting cut to viewers who watched F-S1.1 to 50%.

- **F-S1.3 — "Notes were stealing my close."**
  - Mode: silent-safe (chrome serif on black, no VO needed). Audio optional sub-impact on "stealing."
  - When to use: when the placement is Reels with low sound-on rate. The verb does the violence; the visual finishes it.

- **F-S1.4 — "Best demo I gave: zero notes."**
  - Mode: audio-driven OR silent (works both). Glass card "BEST DEMO" + chrome "0 NOTES" stamp underneath.
  - When to use: when the creative-director wants a tonal contrast to F-S1.1's confessional — this one is a brag-truth, not a confession-truth.

### Category 2 — Reveal / voyeur (mid-call screen exposed)

- **F-R1.1 — [visual-only] AE's screen, Notion doc open, hands typing fast while a Zoom tile in the corner shows the prospect mid-sentence. At 0.9s the typing freezes, the Notion doc fades out, and the Tonvo overlay lights up on the same screen catching the prospect's exact words.**
  - Mode: silent-safe (entirely visual). Optional: faint Zoom-audio buzz under it.
  - When to use: V2-01 cold-open if creative-director prefers visual S0 over verbal S0. Demands a screen-recording asset (see notes au CD).

- **F-R1.2 — [visual-only] Split screen: left half = AE typing furiously in a Google Doc, right half = same call with her hands flat on the desk, Tonvo capturing on-screen. Both clocks read "32:14." Left side has a red "MISSED" pip on the prospect's audio waveform; right side has a green "FLAG" pip.**
  - Mode: silent-safe. Sub-impact at 1.2s when the two pips appear.
  - When to use: contrast frame. Reads in 1.5s without text. Heaviest production cost in the F bank.

- **F-R1.3 — "Watch what my hands did during this close."** + screen rec of frantic two-handed typing
  - Mode: audio-driven (the line is the bait, the screen rec is the payoff). VO over a clean wide of the laptop.
  - When to use: when the body of the ad needs to teach via demonstration (V2-01 in its full 18s form).

- **F-R1.4 — [visual-only] Single iPhone 17 mockup, Tonvo open, transcript scrolling fast in real-time while the AE's actual notepad (paper, beside the phone) stays blank. Pen sits across the blank pad.**
  - Mode: silent-safe. The blank pad IS the hook.
  - When to use: 1:1 Feed format (V2-02 spine). The static blank pad reads even at thumbnail size.

### Category 3 — Confrontation question (challenge the viewer)

- **F-C1.1 — "How much did you type today?"**
  - Mode: audio-driven (low-mid male coach voice, direct, no smirk). On-screen: just a blinking cursor on black.
  - When to use: when retargeting warm viewers who already engaged with V1's $50K POV. The question lands harder when they've already had the loss-aversion frame.

- **F-C1.2 — "Whose call was that — yours or your keyboard's?"**
  - Mode: audio-driven (this one needs the rhythm; silent version loses the punch). 9 words, but the second clause is parenthetical and reads in <0.5s — total parse time stays under the 1.5s window.
  - When to use: when creative-director can afford 1.6s S0 instead of 1.5s. Confirm with timeline before greenlight; if the cut budget is tight, swap to F-C1.1.

- **F-C1.3 — "Why are you still typing?"**
  - Mode: audio-driven OR silent. The 5-word version of F-C1.1, used when the visual carries more weight.
  - When to use: paired with a screen-rec of frantic typing — the visual answers the question by showing the absurdity.

### Category 4 — Pattern interrupt (visual, described, no text)

- **F-P1.1 — [visual-only] First frame: a mechanical keyboard, top-down macro shot, keys are visibly worn on the home row. At 0.4s the keyboard slides off-frame to the right, leaving a clean desk surface and a single iPhone 17 running Tonvo. No words, no overlay.**
  - Mode: silent-safe. Sound design optional: keyboard-slide whoosh.
  - When to use: brand-spot tone. Opens V2-07 if the creative-director wants the umbrella ad to lead with F instead of G.

- **F-P1.2 — [visual-only] AE's hands on the keyboard mid-call. Fingers freeze mid-keystroke. Hold the freeze for 0.6s. Cut to the same hands now flat on the desk, palms down, while the laptop screen behind them shows Tonvo's transcript flowing on its own.**
  - Mode: silent-safe. Sub-impact on the freeze.
  - When to use: when the creative-director wants the "cognitive offload" frame literalized. Hands stop = brain frees.

### Category 5 — Specificity / numbers (sourced or generic-pattern only)

- **F-N1.1 — "8 demos this week. Zero notes."**
  - Mode: silent-safe (chrome numerals on black). Sub-impact on "Zero."
  - When to use: when the audience is AE-primary and "8 demos a week" reads as a real workload pattern (it's a generic AE-life pattern, not a Tonvo metric — does NOT need a source file). The 0 is the punch.

- **F-N1.2 — "47-minute call. Page stayed blank."**
  - Mode: silent-safe. Glass card "00:47:00" with the "PAGE 1" Notion frame beside it, completely empty, cursor blinking on line 1.
  - When to use: when the creative-director wants continuity with V1's "47 minutes" language (per closing-hooks Hook 1.1). The 47 is a generic-illustrative call length, not a Tonvo-metric claim. No source needed.

- **F-N1.3 — [PENDING source] "30% of an AE's call is typing."**
  - Mode: audio-driven (coach voice, factual).
  - When to use: ONLY if `02-research/` produces a dated source on AE typing-load percentage (e.g., a Gong/Chorus public report, a Salesforce State-of-Sales benchmark). Brief §8.2 forbids unsourced metric claims. If no source lands by V2-01 brief signoff, this scroll-stop drops or gets rephrased to F-N1.1's pattern-generic version.

## Angle G · The Signal You Missed, Live

_Mechanic per brief §3: real-time revelation. Tonvo catches what the AE's ear didn't — the hesitation, the hidden objection, the buying-signal phrase. Proof shifts from "imagine" to "watch." Landing match: typewriter line 3 "Without missing a signal."_

### Category 1 — Statement-as-truth

- **G-S2.1 — "Tonvo heard what you missed."**
  - Mode: audio-driven (calm low-mid voice, no excitement — the line lands harder flat than punchy). On-screen: chrome serif lower-third over a Zoom call screen-rec.
  - When to use: V2-03 cold-open. The flat delivery primes the live-demo reveal in S1-S2.

- **G-S2.2 — "Three signals fired in this call."**
  - Mode: audio-driven OR silent. The "three" anchors a curiosity gap — viewer wants to know which three.
  - When to use: paired with V2-03's 3-glass-card payoff at S2-S3. Brief §7 V2-03 calls for exactly 3 glass-card pops; this hook pre-sells the payoff.

- **G-S2.3 — "She said yes. You heard maybe."**
  - Mode: silent-safe (two stacked chrome lines on black). Sub-impact on "maybe."
  - When to use: when the ad-set targets ICP-2 (Top-Rep) — the implication that even good reps mis-hear lands harder than for ICP-1.

### Category 2 — Reveal / voyeur (mid-call screen exposed)

- **G-R2.1 — [visual-only] Single iPhone 17 mockup, Tonvo open on a live call. The prospect's voice waveform scrolls left-to-right at the top; at 0.7s a soft chrome pip lights up on the waveform and a glass card slides in: "BUYING SIGNAL — 'how soon could we start?'" Hold the card to 1.5s.**
  - Mode: silent-safe (the quote on the glass card carries the meaning). Sub-bass on the pip.
  - When to use: V2-03 lead. This is the cleanest "watch the product work" frame. Reuses `assets/demo-session.mp4` per brief §3.

- **G-R2.2 — [visual-only] Zoom call screen, prospect speaking. Tonvo overlay catches a single word in the transcript — "hesitate" — and underlines it in chrome at 0.8s. A small glass tag appears beside it: "HESITATION DETECTED."**
  - Mode: silent-safe. Optional faint Zoom-audio buzz under.
  - When to use: signal-detection literalism. Demands a screen-rec asset with a staged transcript (see CD notes).

- **G-R2.3 — "Watch Tonvo catch the signal — live."** + screen rec
  - Mode: audio-driven. Coach VO over the screen rec.
  - When to use: V2-04 (1:1 Feed reformat). The "live" suffix is the differentiator from V1's retrospective POV ads.

- **G-R2.4 — [visual-only] Split screen: left = AE's face mid-call, eyes scanning, slightly distracted. Right = the same call's audio waveform with a clear spike highlighted, Tonvo glass card naming the spike: "READY-TO-BUY PHRASE." Left side has no reaction; right side has the catch.**
  - Mode: silent-safe. Sub-impact at 1.2s when the glass card lands.
  - When to use: identity-gap frame. Borrows the energy of V1 Angle C ("The Gap") but ports it to live signal-detection.

### Category 3 — Confrontation question

- **G-C2.1 — "What did your buyer just say?"**
  - Mode: audio-driven. Beat of silence after, then a soft Tonvo chime as the answer would appear on-screen in S1.
  - When to use: V2-03 cold-open alternative to G-S2.1. This one provokes; that one states. Pick by ICP — ICP-2 takes provocation, ICP-1 takes statement.

- **G-C2.2 — "How many signals did you miss today?"**
  - Mode: audio-driven (coach voice, the kind that lands in a 1:1 not a townhall).
  - When to use: retargeting layer — viewers who watched V1 closing-sprint signal ads. The question presumes loss; the body delivers the count.

### Category 4 — Pattern interrupt (visual)

- **G-P2.1 — [visual-only] Black screen. A single audio waveform pulses across the middle, flat then a small spike then flat again. At 0.6s a chrome ring snaps around the spike. At 1.0s the ring expands and reveals a glass card behind it: "SIGNAL." No other text, no logo yet.**
  - Mode: silent-safe. Sub-bass on the ring-snap.
  - When to use: V2-07 umbrella ad opener if the creative-director wants G to lead. Cleanest pattern-interrupt in the catalog — reads in feed thumbnail.

- **G-P2.2 — [visual-only] iPhone 17 mockup, Tonvo open. The transcript is scrolling, mostly grey text, but at 0.7s a single phrase ("just one more thing") highlights in chrome and lifts off the screen 2-3px with a soft glass sheen. Hold to 1.5s.**
  - Mode: silent-safe. Optional sub-tap on the lift.
  - When to use: when the creative-director wants the iPhone-mockup mandate (per memory: IPhone17Mockup wraps all Tonvo ads) to do double duty as the pattern-interrupt.

### Category 5 — Specificity / numbers (sourced or generic-pattern)

- **G-N2.1 — "3 buying signals per call. You hear 1."**
  - Mode: silent-safe (chrome 3 and 1 on black, sub-impact on "1"). 8 words exactly — at the cap.
  - When to use: direct reuse of V1 closing-hooks Hook 3.5 pattern, now mounted on G. The 3-vs-1 ratio is a coach-claim about AE attention, not a Tonvo metric — does not need source file. If creative-director or strategist disputes, drop to G-N2.2.

- **G-N2.2 — "One signal. One close. Watch."**
  - Mode: audio-driven OR silent. Three short clauses, total 5 words.
  - When to use: when G-N2.1's "you hear 1" reads as too accusatory for a cold audience. This one is invitational, same numeric anchor.

- **G-N2.3 — [PENDING source] "27% more closes. Same pitch."**
  - Mode: audio-driven.
  - When to use: ONLY as a landing-echo hook tied to the hero copy "Close 27% more deals." The 27% is an approved landing number per brief §8.2 — BUT brief §10 Q3 still asks Emilien where that number traces to. Until §10 Q3 resolves, this hook ships marked PENDING and the creative-director should not lead with it. If §10 Q3 resolves green, this becomes a top-tier umbrella hook (H7 echo per brief §4).

### Frame-accurate timing scroll-stops

These will not survive sloppy cuts. Demand the creative-director call out the frame numbers in the brief:

- **F-R1.1** — typing-freeze must hit exactly 0.9s. Earlier reads as edit-glitch; later misses the 1.5s window.
- **G-R2.1** — pip at 0.7s, glass card lands at 1.0s, hold to 1.5s. The 0.3s gap between pip and card is the "Tonvo just caught it" beat.
- **G-P2.1** — ring-snap at 0.6s sub-bass, ring-expand at 1.0s. The audio cue must land on the snap, not the expand.
- **G-P2.2** — phrase highlights at 0.7s, lifts at the same frame. If the lift lags the highlight by even 2 frames it reads as a bug, not a reveal.
- **F-P1.2** — freeze duration is 0.6s. Shorter = invisible, longer = breaks the 1.5s cap.
